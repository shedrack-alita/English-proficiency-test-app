@topic Concord
@instruction Choose the option that best completes the sentence.
@tip Periods of time and amounts seen as one unit take a singular verb; "minutes" (of a meeting) is plural; names of diseases ending in -s are singular.
So far, three days ____ been spent on this. | *has | have | were | are || Following UI's sample-paper key, a period of time treated as one unit takes "has".
The new army barracks ____ close to my house. | are | *is | were | been || "The new army barracks" here is one military base, so the verb is singular.
@tip Titles of games, books and films are singular even when they look plural.
Snakes and Ladders ____ interesting. | *is | are | were | have been || "Snakes and Ladders" is the name of one game, so it takes the singular "is".
@tip Amounts of liquid, money or time viewed as one quantity take a singular verb.
Ten litres of petrol ____ enough. | *is | are | were | have been || "Ten litres of petrol" is one quantity, so "is".
@tip The name of a firm is singular even when it contains "and".
Omoope and Associates ____ my first choice for this contract. | *is | are | were | have been || "Omoope and Associates" is the name of one firm, so "is".
@tip Subjects joined by "and" take a plural verb, unless they name one person or one thing.
Dr Eben, Jesse and Emmanuel ____ to play. | loves | *love | is loving | has loved || Three people joined by "and" form a plural subject, so "love".
His sister and confidant ____ arrived. | have | *has | are | were || "His sister and confidant" is one person (his sister, who is also his confidant), so the verb is singular.
Yam and egg ____ my favourite meal. | *is | are | were | have been || "Yam and egg" is one dish, so it takes the singular "is".
@tip Third-person singular subjects take -s in the present simple; plural subjects do not.
Ben ____ a lot. | talk | *talks | are talking | have talked || "Ben" is third-person singular, so "talks".
The boys ____ in the choir. | sings | *sing | is singing | has sung || "The boys" is plural, so the verb has no -s.
@tip Irregular plurals (mice, feet, teeth, children, people) take plural verbs.
The mice ____ dead. | is | *are | was | has been || "Mice" is the plural of "mouse", so "are".
My feet ____ cold. | is | *are | was | has been || "Feet" is plural, so "are".
@tip In "one of the ... who ...", the verb after "who" agrees with the plural noun.
The retired military officer is one of the past leaders who ____ the embodiment of corruption. | have | has | is | *are || "Who" refers to "the past leaders" (plural), so the verb in the relative clause is "are".
@tip "Each of", "either of" and "neither of" are followed by a plural noun and a singular verb.
Each of the ____ must wait behind. | girl | *girls | girl's | girls' || After "each of", the noun is plural ("the girls"), although the verb that follows is singular.
@tip With "neither ... nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
Neither Dr Whales nor his friends ____ how to swim. | knows | *know | is knowing | has known || The nearer subject, "his friends", is plural, so "know".
@tip "Every" makes a compound subject singular.
Every cow and goat ____ grass. | eat | *eats | are eating | have eaten || "Every" makes the subject singular: every cow and (every) goat eats grass.
@tip "All but X" means "all except X", and the verb agrees with "all" (plural for people).
All but Admin ____ asleep. | is | *are | was | has been || The subject is "all" (everyone except Admin), which is plural.
@tip With "either ... or", the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
Either Celxius or Squpho ____ coming for the ceremony. | are | *is | were | have been || Two singular subjects joined by "either ... or" take a singular verb.
@tip "Much of" + uncountable noun takes a singular verb.
Much of the fuel I bought ____ evaporated. | have | *has | are | were || "Much of the fuel" is an uncountable quantity, so "has".
@tip After "I pray that", "I insist that" and similar, formal English uses the subjunctive (base form, no -s).
I pray that God ____ you with your struggles. | helps | *help | helped | is helping || After "pray that", formal English uses the subjunctive base form: "God help you".
@tip Indefinite pronouns such as everyone, everybody, someone, no one, nothing take singular verbs.
Everyone ____ annoying me. | are | *is | were | have been || "Everyone" is singular, so "is".
@tip With "or", the verb agrees with the subject closest to it.
If Akinyele passes the EPT, Admin, Dr Whales, Dosunmu or his friends ____ to thank. | is | *are | was | has || The nearest subject, "his friends", is plural, so "are".
@tip "A pair of" + plural noun takes a singular verb, because the head word is "pair".
A pair of trousers ____ on the bed. | are | *is | were | have been || The head of the subject is "pair" (singular), so "is".
@tip Phrases such as "together with" and "as well as" do not change the number of the subject.
The manager together with her workers ____ lazy. | are | *is | were | have been || The subject is "the manager" (singular); "together with her workers" is an added phrase.
@tip When a subject is followed by "not ...", the verb agrees with the affirmative subject.
The police, not the criminal, ____ to blame. | is | *are | was | has been || The verb agrees with "the police" (always plural); "not the criminal" is an aside.
@tip In "one of the ... who ...", the verb after "who" agrees with the plural noun.
One of the elders who ____ generously was honoured. | gives | *give | is giving | has given || "Who" refers to "the elders" (plural), so the verb in the relative clause is "give".
@tip "Series" referring to one set of things is singular.
The series of killings ____ me scared. | make | *makes | are making | have made || "The series" (one sequence of killings) is singular, so "makes".
@tip "Most of" + plural noun takes a plural verb.
Most of the issues ____ internal. | is | *are | was | has been || "Most of the issues" refers to plural issues, so "are".
@tip "Each" and indefinite pronouns are singular; plural numbers ("four of my friends") are plural.
One of the robbers ____ escaped. | *has | have | are | were || The subject is "one" (of the robbers), which is singular, so "has".
Four of my friends ____ coming. | is | *are | was | has || "Four of my friends" is plural, so "are".
@tip "Means" (a method) has the same form in singular and plural; "this means" is singular.
This means of transport ____ the best. | *is | are | were | have been || "This means" refers to one method of transport, so "is".
@tip Collective nouns (family, staff, majority) take a plural verb when the members are thought of as individuals.
All the members of the family ____ excited. | is | *are | was | has been || "All the members" is plural, so "are".
The staff ____ earned many awards this year. | has | *have | is | was || "The staff" here refers to the individual employees who earned awards, so British usage takes the plural "have".
The majority of people ____ interested in sport. | is | *are | was | has been || "The majority of people" refers to many individuals, so the verb is plural.
Minority parties ____ grown over the past two years. | has | *have | is | was || "Minority parties" is plural, so "have".
Members of the navy ____ called sailors. | is | *are | was | has been || "Members" is the plural head of the subject.
@tip Indefinite pronouns ending in -body, -one and -thing are singular, as is "anywhere" used as a subject.
Everybody ____ her. | like | *likes | are liking | have liked || "Everybody" is singular, so "likes".
No one ____ tomorrow. | know | *knows | are knowing | have known || "No one" is singular, so "knows".
Everything here ____ beautiful. | are | *is | were | have been || "Everything" is singular, so "is".
Anywhere you take me ____ fine by me. | are | *is | were | have been || The noun clause "anywhere you take me" acts as a single subject, so "is".
Somebody ____ them the secret. | tell | *tells | are telling | have told || "Somebody" is singular, so "tells".
@tip "More than one" + singular noun takes a singular verb.
More than one member ____ protested against the proposal. | *has | have | are | were || "More than one member" takes a singular verb.
@tip Fractions take the number of the noun after "of": "two-thirds of the area" (singular area) is singular.
Two-thirds of the area ____ under water. | are | *is | were | have been || "Area" is singular, so "two-thirds of the area is".
@tip Plural subjects take the base form of the verb in the present simple.
Medical research shows that measles ____ a person only once in a lifetime. | attack | *attacks | attacked | attacking || "Measles" is the name of a disease and is singular.

@topic Discourse Reference
@instruction Identify the word or expression that the bold word(s) refer to.
@tip Summary nouns ("fact", "issue", "problem") refer back to a whole statement. Pick the option that restates exactly what the earlier sentence claims.
Messi has won five Ballon d'Or awards and some see him as the best. This is a **fact**. | He has won five Ballon d'Or awards | He is the best | Messi | *He is the best and has won five Ballon d'Or awards || The study group's key takes "this" as referring to the whole preceding sentence. (Strictly, only the five awards are a fact; "the best" is an opinion.)
@tip "The former" refers to the first of two things mentioned; "the latter" to the second.
I just got a new toothbrush, unlike **the former**. | New toothbrush | *Old toothbrush | Bad toothbrush | Latter toothbrush || "The former" here means the earlier (old) toothbrush, contrasted with the new one.
@tip Negative replies with "any" or "either" pick up the items just mentioned.
Lightoj can speak Yoruba and French; I can't speak **any**. | Can speak neither | *Can't speak either | Can speak Yoruba | Can speak English || "I can't speak any" refers to the two languages mentioned, meaning "I can't speak either of them".
@tip A pronoun can point forward (cataphora) to a noun phrase that comes later.
Oh! There **you** are, my cute little kitten. | My kitten | Little kitten | *Cute little kitten | Cute kitten || "You" points forward to the vocative "my cute little kitten"; of the options, (c) gives the full phrase.
@tip Quantity expressions such as "many more" are interpreted against an amount already mentioned.
I gave out a lot of my cutlery; **many more** were returned to me. | *More than what I gave | As much as I gave | A few more than I gave | Exactly what I gave || "Many more" (than I gave out) means more pieces came back than were given.
@tip Words like "round neck" can stand for a noun from the question (ellipsis): "my black round neck (shirt)".
Betty: Which shirt are you wearing this morning? Dr Whales: I will wear my black **round neck**. | Black shirt | Black | *Shirt | Casual shirt || "Round neck" describes the shirt; the noun "shirt" is understood from the question.
@tip In ellipsis, part of a clause is left out because it can be recovered. Rebuild the full clause.
I could have beaten her, but I decided **not to**. | *I didn't beat her | I would not do such a thing | I almost beat her | Indecisive about beating her || "Not to" stands for "not to beat her", so the meaning is that I did not beat her.
@tip "A few" after a list means "some among many".
Tobitools, Blaise, Niel, Honfikky and Modepeayo are active members on the page, just to mention a **few**. | Not many are active | Many and few are active | *These are some names among the many active members | These are the names of the most active members || "Just to mention a few" signals that the names listed are only some of the many active members.
@tip A pronoun refers back to the noun it matches and makes sense with.
Phemmy is always honest with his scores on the page; **he** doesn't lie. | Honest person | Truthful Phemmy | *Phemmy | Sincere person || "He" refers to Phemmy. The other options add descriptions not in the text.

@topic Lexis and Structure
@instruction Choose the option that best completes the sentence.
@tip Use "a" before a consonant sound and "an" before a vowel sound: "a European" (starts with a "y" sound), "an honest" (silent h).
Ben works for ____ European company. | *a | an | any | the || "European" begins with a "y" sound, so it takes "a".
____ honest man is hard to find. | a | *an | some | the || "Honest" begins with a silent "h" and a vowel sound, so it takes "an".
@tip After a verb or preposition, use object pronouns: me, him, her, us, them.
The dog chased Ojo and ____. | *me | I | myself | mine || "Me" is the object of "chased".
Those men helped Bola and ____. | I | *me | myself | mine || "Me" is the object of "helped". (The group's key gave "I", which is wrong after a verb.)
@tip Some abbreviations hide a word: "o'clock" is short for "of the clock".
In "10 o'clock", the "o" stands for | on | at | *of | off || "O'clock" is a contraction of "of the clock".
@tip Many adjectives, nouns and verbs take a fixed preposition. Learn them as pairs: insist on, aversion to, good at, preside over, convicted of, deal in, translate into.
He is insistent ____ doing the job. | in | *on | at | with || "Insistent on" (like "insist on") is the standard combination.
Bilikisu has shown enough aversion ____ science. | *to | for | with | on || The standard combination is "aversion to". (The group's key gave "for".)
Do you know how good he is ____ Maths? | *at | in | on | for || We are good "at" a subject or activity.
They are worse ____ physics than we thought. | in | *at | for | with || "Worse at" follows the same pattern as "good at".
The boy was charged ____ attempted theft. | for | of | against | *with || One is "charged with" an offence.
He was lucky he was not convicted ____ the crime. | with | *of | on | for || One is "convicted of" a crime. (The group's key gave "for".)
Their father deals ____ expensive shoes. | on | *in | at | for || To "deal in" goods is to buy and sell them.
The teacher decided to translate his lectures ____ the local languages. | to | *into | in | within || One translates "into" another language.
@tip Possessive pronouns (its, yours, hers, ours, theirs) never take an apostrophe.
The dog broke ____ leg. | it's | *its | its' | it || "Its" is the possessive; "it's" means "it is".
The bag isn't mine but ____. | *yours | your's | yours' | your own || The possessive pronoun "yours" has no apostrophe.
@tip "Hang" has two past forms: "hung" for objects, "hanged" only for executing a person.
The man ____ his clothes on the wire. | hang | *hung | hanged | spread || Clothes are "hung". "Hanged" is only used for executing a person. (The group's key gave "hanged".)
@tip Some nouns are uncountable in English (furniture, equipment, information, luggage) and some appear only in the plural (arms meaning weapons, amends).
The army ran short of ____. | arm and ammunitions | arms and ammunitions | *arms and ammunition | arm and ammunition || "Arms" (weapons) is always plural, and "ammunition" is uncountable.
We left a lot of ____ at the factory. | furniture and equipments | furnitures and equipments | furnitures and equipment | *furniture and equipment || "Furniture" and "equipment" are uncountable and have no plural form.
We promise to make ____. | amend | ammends | ammend | *amends || The fixed expression is "make amends", with one "m" and a final "s".
@tip "The + adjective" (the helpless, the poor, the rich) refers to a group of people and takes a plural verb.
The helpless ____ our sympathy. | *require | requires | does require | does not require || "The helpless" means helpless people, so the verb is plural: "require".
@tip A number + measurement used as an adjective before a noun stays singular and is hyphenated: "a fifteen-litre jar".
Their father gave us six fifteen-____ jars. | litres | *litre | litres' | litre's || In a compound adjective ("fifteen-litre jars"), the measurement word stays singular.
@tip A noun used to describe another noun can be possessive plural: "the students' union" (the union of the students).
The president of the ____ union has been rusticated. | student | Student's | *students' | students || It is the union belonging to the students, so the plural possessive "students' union".
@tip "Few" (hardly any) is negative; "a few" (some) is positive. "Little/a little" work the same way with uncountable nouns.
The politician hardly visits anybody; he trusts ____ of his associates. | *few | a few | a little | little || "Hardly visits anybody" suggests he trusts almost none of them: "few" (not many). "Associates" is countable, so not "little".
@tip Progressive passive: "are being" + past participle; after "was", "being" + past participle.
All the four boys ____ detained. | was being | is being | has been | *are being || "The four boys" is plural, so "are being detained".
Ballanda was ____ deceived by the magician. | *being | been | be | beings || The past continuous passive is "was being deceived".
@tip Learn the principal parts of irregular verbs: burst-burst-burst, grind-ground-ground, shake-shook-shaken, freeze-froze-frozen, flee-fled-fled, sew-sewed-sewn.
Tunji has ____ the idea of going overseas. | bursts | *burst | bursted | bursting || "Burst" has the same form in the past participle ("has burst"). (Strictly, one "bursts" a bubble; the item tests the verb form.)
Have you ____ the pepper at the shop? | grind | grinded | grounded | *ground || The past participle of "grind" is "ground".
He has ____ hands with her. | shakes | shook | *shaken | shaked || After "has", the past participle "shaken" is needed. (The group's key gave "shook", which is the past simple.)
The mother ____ the fish in the refrigerator. | frozen | freezed | *froze | freeze || The past simple of "freeze" is "froze".
The criminals ____ as soon as the killer shot the man. | flees | fleed | *fled | flee || The past of "flee" is "fled".
She hasn't ____ any dress yet. | sowed | sew | *sewn | sown || The past participle of "sew" is "sewn" ("sown" is from "sow", to plant seed).
@tip Learn fixed expressions as wholes: "at times", "put in an appearance", "pool resources".
She usually works hard, but ____ she can be very lazy. | *at times | a times | at time | at timings || "At times" means "sometimes".
Despite the pressure of work during the week, I shall endeavour to ____ at your wedding. | Put up an appearance | Put up appearance | *Put in an appearance | Put on an appearance || The idiom is "put in an appearance", meaning to attend briefly.
The exercise will be easily accomplished by the team if members ____ their ____. | Pull/resources together | Pool/resources together | Pull/resources | *Pool/resources || To combine resources is to "pool" them; "pool their resources" needs no "together". (The group's key gave option c.)
