@instruction Choose the correct answer to complete the sentence.
@tip General truths and facts take the present simple ("Water boils at 100°C").
Water ____ at a temperature of 100°C. | is to boil | is boiling | *boils | boiled || A scientific fact is expressed with the present simple: "boils".
@topic Lexis and Structure
@tip Weather statements use "it" as a dummy subject: "It is very hot", "It rains".
In some countries ____ very hot all the time. | there is | is | *it is | they are || Statements about weather need the dummy subject "it": "it is very hot".
@tip To express purpose, use "to" + verb (the infinitive of purpose), not "for to" or "for -ing".
In cold countries people wear thick clothes ____ warm. | for keeping | *to keep | for to keep | keeping for || Purpose is expressed with the infinitive: "to keep warm".
@tip "The weather" takes the definite article when we mean the weather in general conversation.
In England people are always talking about ____. | a weather | *the weather | weather | weathers || We talk about "the weather"; "weather" is uncountable and takes "the" here.
In some places ____ almost every day. | *it rains | there rains | it raining | rains it || Weather verbs take the dummy subject "it": "it rains".
@tip "Any" is used in negative sentences and questions; "some" in positive statements.
In deserts there isn't ____ grass. | the | some | *any | no || In a negative sentence ("isn't"), use "any".
@tip "Weather" is uncountable, so it never takes "a".
Places near the Equator have ____ weather even in the cold season. | a warm | the warm | *warm | warms || "Weather" is uncountable, so no article: "warm weather".
@tip Superlatives take "the": the coldest, the best, the most important.
In England ____ time of year is usually from December to February. | coldest | *the coldest | colder | most cold || A superlative referring to one particular period takes "the": "the coldest time".
@tip "Most people" (general) has no "of"; "most of the people" (a specific group) needs "of the".
____ people don't know what it's like in other countries. | The most | Most of | *Most | Mostly || "Most people" refers to people in general and needs no "of".
@tip "Few" goes with countable plurals (people), "little" with uncountables; "very less" is not English.
Very ____ people can travel abroad. | less | little | *few | fewer of || "People" is countable, so "very few people".
@topic Tense and Aspect
@tip A finished event at a stated past time (in 1960) takes the past simple, not the present perfect.
Mohammed Ali ____ his first world title fight in 1960. | has won | *won | is winning | had been winning || A completed event at a definite past time ("in 1960") takes the past simple.
@tip For an action completed before another past action, use the past perfect ("had won").
After he ____ an Olympic gold medal he became a professional boxer. | *had won | have won | was winning | has won || Winning the medal happened before he became a professional, so the past perfect "had won".
@topic Lexis and Structure
@tip "Make someone do something" takes the bare infinitive (no "to").
His religious beliefs ____ his name when he became champion. | have made him | made him to change | *made him change | made him changing || "Make" + object + bare infinitive: "made him change".
@topic Tense and Aspect
@tip Third conditional (unreal past): "If + had + past participle, ... would have + past participle".
If he ____ his first fight with Sonny Liston, no one would have been surprised. | has | would have | *had | has had || The main clause "would have been" signals the third conditional, so "if he had lost".
@topic Lexis and Structure
@tip "Both ... and" is a correlative pair; don't mix it with "or".
He has travelled a lot ____ as a boxer and as a world-famous personality. | *both | and | or | either || "Both ... and" introduces two parallel items: both as a boxer and as a personality.
He is very well known ____ the world. | all in | *all over | in all | over all || The idiom is "all over the world".
@topic Tense and Aspect
@tip Verbs of belief and opinion (believe, know, think) are not normally used in the continuous form.
Many people ____ he was the greatest boxer of all time. | is believing | are believing | *believe | believes || "Believe" is a stative verb and takes the simple form; "many people" is plural.
@topic Lexis and Structure
@tip After a superlative, use "in" for places or groups: "the best in the world".
To be the best ____ the world is not easy. | from | *in | of | at || After a superlative, "in" is used with a place: "the best in the world".
@tip "Must" has no past form; use "had to" for past obligation.
Like any top sportsman, Ali ____ train very hard. | *had to | must | should | ought || The sentence describes a past necessity, and "must" has no past form, so "had to".
@topic Tense and Aspect
@tip "Will" expresses a prediction about the future.
Even though he has now lost his title, people ____ always remember him as a champion. | would | *will | did | have || A confident prediction about the future takes "will".

@nopassage
@tip "Say" is not followed by a personal object ("say that"); "tell" is ("tell someone that").
Many teachers ____ their students should learn a foreign language. | say to | *say | tell | speak || "Say" takes a clause directly ("say (that) their students should ..."); "tell" would need an object and "that".
@tip The pattern is "the same as", not "the same like" or "the same than".
Learning a second language is not the same ____ learning a first language. | *as | like | than | to || "The same" is followed by "as".
It takes ____ to learn any language. | long time | long | *a long time | the long time || The expression is "it takes a long time".
@tip Superlatives of short adjectives use "-est": hard, harder, the hardest.
It is said that Chinese is the world's ____ language to master. | harder | *hardest | more hard | most hard || A superlative is needed: "the world's hardest language".
@tip "Which" or "that" introduces a relative clause about things; "who" is for people.
English is quite difficult because of all the exceptions ____ have to be learnt. | who | *which | what | whom || "Which" refers to the exceptions (things).
@tip "Be willing to" expresses readiness; "want" is rarely used in the continuous form.
You can learn the basic structures of a language quite quickly, but only if you ____ make an effort. | are wanting | will to | *are willing to | willing || "Are willing to" means "are ready to".
@tip "Be used to" (accustomed to) is followed by an -ing form.
A lot of people aren't used ____ grammar in their own language. | to the study | to study | *to studying | studying || After "be used to", use the -ing form: "used to studying".
@tip "Wish" about the past takes the past perfect: "I wish I had started earlier".
Many adult students wish they ____ their language studies earlier. | would start | would have started | *had started | have started || A wish about a past situation takes the past perfect.
@tip "On one's own" means alone, without help.
In some countries students have to spend a lot of time working ____ their own. | *on | by | in | for || The idiom is "on their own".
There aren't ____ easy ways of learning a foreign language in your own country. | no | *any | some | none || "Aren't" is already negative, so "any" (not "no").
@tip "Listen" needs "to" before its object; "hear" does not.
Some people try to improve their English by ____ the BBC World Service. | hearing | listening | *listening to | hearing to || "Listen" takes "to" before an object.
@tip A gerund (-ing form) can be the subject of a sentence.
____ with a foreign family can be a good way to learn a language. | Live | Life | *Living | To living || The subject is a gerund: "Living with a foreign family".
@tip "It's no use" is followed by an -ing form.
It's no use ____ to learn a language just by studying a dictionary. | to try | *trying | in trying | try || The expression is "it's no use doing something".
@tip "Would rather" is followed by the bare infinitive; the negative is "would rather not".
Many students ____ take tests. | *would rather not | would rather prefer not | would rather not to | would not rather || The negative of "would rather" is "would rather not" + base verb.
@tip "It's time" + past tense means "it should happen now".
Some people think it's time we all ____ a single international language. | learn | should learn | *learnt | had learnt || After "it's time we ...", the past simple is used for the present.

@topic Tag Questions
@instruction Choose the correct question tag.
@nopassage
@tip Work out the auxiliary in the statement (is, has, will, would, had) and repeat it in the tag with the opposite polarity. "'s" can be "is" or "has": check the next word.
John's coming to see you, ____? | hasn't he | wasn't he | *isn't he | doesn't he || "John's coming" = "John is coming", so the tag is "isn't he?"
It's been a long time since you've seen him, ____? | *hasn't it | isn't it | haven't you | wasn't it || "It's been" = "it has been", so the tag is "hasn't it?"
He's due to arrive tomorrow, ____? | won't he | *isn't he | will he | doesn't he || "He's due" = "he is due", so the tag repeats "is": "isn't he?"
He won't be getting in till about 10.30, ____? | isn't he | is he | *will he | won't he || The statement is negative ("won't"), so the tag is positive: "will he?"
You met him while you were on holiday, ____? | *didn't you | weren't you | haven't you | don't you || "Met" is past simple, so the tag uses "did": "didn't you?"
I think I'm expected to pick him up, ____? | *aren't I | don't I | are you | am I || The tag follows the reported clause "I'm expected", so "aren't I?"
No doubt you'd rather he stayed in England now, ____? | didn't you | *wouldn't you | shouldn't you | hadn't you || "You'd rather" = "you would rather", so the tag is "wouldn't you?"
Nobody else has been told he's coming, ____? | is he | has he | *have they | haven't they || "Nobody" makes the statement negative, so the tag is positive, and it takes "they": "have they?"
We'd better not stay up too late tonight, ____? | didn't we | have we | *had we | hadn't we || "We'd better" = "we had better"; the statement is negative, so "had we?"
I suppose it's time we called it a day, ____? | didn't we | *isn't it | don't I | wasn't it || The tag follows the main idea "it's time", so "isn't it?"
