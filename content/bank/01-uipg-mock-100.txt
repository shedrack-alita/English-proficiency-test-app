@tip Ask what job the word does in this sentence: naming (noun), describing a noun (adjective), describing a verb or adjective (adverb), linking (conjunction), showing relation (preposition) or expressing emotion (interjection).

@topic Parallelism
@instruction Choose from the options a-d the expression that represents the most acceptable parallel structure.
@tip Items joined by "and", "or" or listed in a series must share the same grammatical form: all adjectives, all -ing forms, all "to" infinitives, and so on.
 | *She is intelligent, hardworking and honest. | She is intelligent, hardworking and has honesty. | She is intelligent, works hard and honest. | She is intelligent, hardworking and being honest. || All three items are adjectives after "is": intelligent, hardworking, honest. The other options switch to a verb phrase or a noun.
 | Tunde enjoys reading novels, to write poems and watching films. | *Tunde enjoys reading novels, writing poems and watching films. | Tunde enjoys to read novels, writing poems and watching films. | Tunde enjoys reading novels, writing poems and to watch films. || "Enjoy" is followed by -ing forms, and the series stays parallel: reading, writing, watching.
@tip When one "to" introduces a series, either use it once before the first item or repeat it before every item, and keep every verb in its base form.
 | The manager promised to increase salaries, to reduce working hours and improving working conditions. | The manager promised increasing salaries, to reduce working hours and improve conditions. | *The manager promised to increase salaries, reduce working hours and improve working conditions. | The manager promised to increase salaries, reduce working hours and to improving conditions. || One "to" governs three base verbs: (to) increase, reduce, improve. The other options mix infinitives with -ing forms.
 | The new laboratory is spacious, well-equipped and it has good ventilation. | The new laboratory is spacious, equipped well and with ventilation. | The new laboratory is spacious, well-equipped and ventilation is good. | *The new laboratory is spacious, well-equipped and well-ventilated. || Three adjectives describe the laboratory: spacious, well-equipped, well-ventilated. The others end with a clause or phrase instead of an adjective.
@tip Items in a series must share the same grammatical form: all adjectives, all -ing forms, all "to" infinitives, and so on.
 | Ade's duties include marking scripts, setting questions and to supervise projects. | *Ade's duties include marking scripts, setting questions and supervising projects. | Ade's duties include to mark scripts, setting questions and supervising projects. | Ade's duties include marking scripts, to set questions and supervising projects. || Each duty is an -ing phrase: marking scripts, setting questions, supervising projects.
 | *The lecture was long, boring and tiring. | The lecture was long, boring and it was tiring. | The lecture was long, bored and tiring. | The lecture was long, boring and tire. || Three adjectives describe the lecture. "Bored" describes a person's feeling, not the lecture, and "tire" is a verb.
@tip In comparisons with "would rather ... than", both sides take the base form of the verb, without "to" or -ing.
 | She would rather walk to school than to take a taxi. | She would rather walk to school than taking a taxi. | *She would rather walk to school than take a taxi. | She would rather walking to school than take a taxi. || "Would rather" is followed by the bare infinitive on both sides of "than": walk ... take.
@tip "Neither" is paired with "nor", and the word after "nor" must match the word after "neither".
The speaker was neither rude ____. | *nor arrogant | or arrogant | or being arrogant | nor arrogance || "Neither" pairs with "nor", and "arrogant" is an adjective like "rude", keeping the structure parallel.
